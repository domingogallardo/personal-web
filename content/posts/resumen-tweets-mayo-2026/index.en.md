## May 28, 2026

> The problem of the supposed consciousness of models is going to become bigger and bigger. The tendency to anthropomorphize them is now joined by Anthropic’s arguments, and others’, that abstractions and feelings may evolve during learning.
>
> Let us be very clear: what evolves through learning is still symbols and correlations. In the same way that an LLM that learns from language can never generate an image of the color red, an LLM will never generate feelings or experience them. The only thing it will be able to do is simulate them.

{{< tweet url="https://x.com/domingogallardo/status/2059872968423936196" >}}

<p>❄ ❄ ❄ ❄ ❄</p>

> Unlike Prof. Elliot, I think it is important that models be able to assess students. Even interactively, by asking questions and proposing exercises to check their level.
>
> A good tutor must be able to detect gaps and mistakes in students, and to change the level and the proposed tasks on the fly, adapting to the student.
>
> I have no problem with AI tutors helping my students study. On the contrary, I think it is the only way to offer personalized attention to hundreds of students.
>
> The teacher will be responsible for guiding the AI tutors on the syllabus and the objectives. And for supervising and validating all the interactions.
>
> And, most importantly, the teacher must guide students in the proper use of their AI tutors. Students must realize that these are just another tool and, most importantly, that they themselves remain ultimately responsible for their own learning. In the end, they are the ones who must decide whether or not they put in the necessary effort.
